## 3. CAC: CPI, funnel rates, cost per retained user

### CPI benchmarks (dating category)

Numbers conflict across trackers — ranges below, with source basis:

- **Adjust dating-category CPI (global): $1.37 (2023) → $1.46 (2024) → $2.76 (2025)**; dating CPM $3.36 (2023) → $4.37 (2024) → $8.57 (2025); CPC $0.18 → $0.48 (2024→2025) [SOFT — Adjust platform data, global average so it blends cheap geos]. Sources: [Adjust Valentine's Day app trends, 2025](https://www.adjust.com/blog/valentines-day-app-trends-2025/) and [Adjust State of Dating Apps, 2026](https://www.adjust.com/blog/state-of-dating-apps/). Caveat: Adjust's pages rate-limited my direct fetch; these figures come from the pages as surfaced in search results — treat as accurate to the source but verify before putting in a deck.
- **US/North America premium:** North America has the highest all-category average CPI, **~$5.30**; all-category iOS ≈ $4.70 vs Android ≈ $3.70 [SOFT] ([Business of Apps CPI research, 2025](https://www.businessofapps.com/ads/cpi/research/cost-per-install/)). One UA-agency benchmark puts **US dating-app CPI around $6.30** [SOFT] ([Udonis UA cost guide, 2026](https://www.blog.udonis.co/user-acquisition/cost)).
- **Apple Search Ads:** overall CPT averaged **~$2.50 in 2025** (up from $1.59 in 2023), overall CPA ~$2.90; dating keywords commonly **$0.50–$6.00 CPT, contested terms $8–12+** [SOFT] ([App Developer Magazine / Apple Ads benchmarks report, 2025](https://appdevelopermagazine.com/apple-ads-search-results-benchmarks-report-2025/); [AppTweak Apple Ads benchmarks, 2026](https://www.apptweak.com/en/aso-blog/apple-ads-benchmarks); [MBADV Apple Ads cost guide, 2026](https://www.mbadv.agency/apple-ads/how-much-do-apple-ads-cost)).
- **Channel-level dating CAC (blog-grade, treat loosely):** USA $4–8/registered user with tight targeting, $12–25 broad; social ads $15–40/user, Google $20–60, influencer $10–30, referral $5–15 [SOFT] ([DatingPro metrics guide](https://www.datingpro.com/blog/key-metrics-for-your-dating-startup-from-cac-to-ltv-and-retention/); [Appetiser CAC case study](https://appetiser.com.au/blog/customer-acquisition-cost-for-apps/)).
- **Platform policy constraint (matters for CAC):** Meta requires **prior written permission** to run dating ads at all, and bans hookup/casual-encounter positioning ([Meta Transparency Center dating-ads policy](https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/restricted-goods-services/dating-ads/)); TikTok requires explicit sales-rep approval for dating ads and bans the category in ~15 markets ([TikTok Advertising Policies](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/tiktok-advertising-policies)). Practically, new dating apps skew to Apple Search Ads, Google UAC, influencer/UGC, and organic/referral.
- **NYC/major-metro premium: no published NYC-specific dating CPI found.** Direction only: North America is the most expensive region (~$5.30 all-category CPI, above) and tier-1 metros price above national average; assume **US-dating CPI $5–8+ for NYC/DMV paid social** as a planning range [EST].

### Funnel rates (install → registration → retained)

- **Install → activation (registration): ~25% on day 1**, 7% incremental by day 30 [SOFT] ([Adjust State of Dating Apps, 2026](https://www.adjust.com/blog/state-of-dating-apps/)).
- **Day-1 retention: 24% (2024) → 26% (2025)**; **Day-30 retention: 5% (2024) → 6% (2025)** (Adjust, same source). Statista/enable3 report harsher D30 for dating: **~2% Android / ~2.7% iOS** [SOFT] ([enable3 retention benchmarks, 2025](https://enable3.io/blog/app-retention-benchmarks-2025); [Statista Android retention by category, 2024](https://www.statista.com/statistics/259329/ios-and-android-app-user-retention-rate/)). Use 2–6% D30 as the honest band.
- **Store conversion (page view → install): ~20% Google Play / ~18.2% iOS** for dating [SOFT] (surfaced via [Business of Apps dating benchmarks, 2026](https://www.businessofapps.com/data/dating-app-benchmarks/) — page blocked my direct fetch, figure from search excerpt).

### Effective cost per retained user [EST — my computation]

cost per D30-retained user = CPI ÷ D30 retention:

| CPI | D30 = 6% (Adjust) | D30 = 15% (strong niche app) |
|---|---|---|
| $1.50 | $25 | $10 |
| $2.76 (2025 global dating avg) | $46 | $18.40 |
| $5.00 (US paid social, realistic) | $83 | $33 |
| $8.00 (NYC premium, high end) | $133 | $53 |

Cost per *registered* user ≈ CPI ÷ 0.25 ≈ 4× CPI (using Adjust's day-1 activation rate).

## 4. LTV:CAC and payback norms

- **3:1 LTV:CAC** is the classic floor; consumer-subscription investors increasingly want **4:1–5:1** [SOFT] ([Adapty CAC benchmarks, 2025](https://adapty.io/blog/customer-acquisition-cost/); [Eightx LTV:CAC guide, 2026](https://eightx.co/blog/ltv-cac-ratio-guide)).
- **CAC payback:** <12 months is the general standard; for competitive consumer subscription apps the target is **3–6 months** because retention curves are steep [SOFT] ([DigitalApplied SaaS unit economics reference, 2026](https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/saas-unit-economics-2026-cac-ltv-payback-reference)).
- **Dating-specific LTV:CAC published benchmarks: not found.** Match/Bumble/Grindr do not disclose CAC. The above are cross-category consumer-subscription norms.

## 7. Simple unit-economics model [EST — all mine, from the assumptions stated]

### Formulas

```
Blended revenue per MAU per month   = conv × ARPPU
LTV per retained user               = conv × ARPPU × L          (L = 12-mo avg payer lifetime, per spec)
Max CAC per retained user @ 3:1     = LTV ÷ 3 = conv × ARPPU × 4
Max affordable CPI                  = Max CAC per retained user × D30 retention
```

"conv" here = probability a retained user becomes a payer, applied at MAU level (conservative — see note below).

### Table A — Blended revenue per free MAU per month (and per 10,000 MAU)

| conv \ ARPPU | $15 | $25 | $35 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2% | $0.30 ($3.0k/mo per 10k MAU) | $0.50 ($5.0k) | $0.70 ($7.0k) |
| 4% | $0.60 ($6.0k) | $1.00 ($10.0k) | $1.40 ($14.0k) |
| 6% | $0.90 ($9.0k) | $1.50 ($15.0k) | $2.10 ($21.0k) |

Sanity check: the 6%/$25–35 cells ($1.50–2.10/MAU/mo) ≈ Grindr/Bumble-level blended ARPU — i.e., top-decile outcome, not a base case.

### Table B — LTV per retained user → max CAC at 3:1 (12-mo payer lifetime)

| conv \ ARPPU | $15 | $25 | $35 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2% | LTV $3.60 → **max CAC $1.20** | $6.00 → **$2.00** | $8.40 → **$2.80** |
| 4% | $7.20 → **$2.40** | $12.00 → **$4.00** | $16.80 → **$5.60** |
| 6% | $10.80 → **$3.60** | $18.00 → **$6.00** | $25.20 → **$8.40** |

### Table C — Max affordable CPI (max CAC × D30 retention)

| conv / ARPPU scenario | Max CAC/retained user | Max CPI @ D30=6% | Max CPI @ D30=15% |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2% / $15 (bear) | $1.20 | $0.07 | $0.18 |
| 4% / $25 (base) | $4.00 | $0.24 | $0.60 |
| 6% / $35 (bull) | $8.40 | $0.50 | $1.26 |

**Compare to actual dating CPIs: $2.76 global (2025) and ~$5–8 US paid social.** Even the bull case supports only ~$0.50–1.26 CPI.

### What this means (the honest conclusion)

1. **Paid-install acquisition at category-average funnel rates does not pencil for a new dating app.** This is not a modeling artifact — it's why no dating app has ever been built primarily on paid social. Incumbents' payers are overwhelmingly organic/re-activated users.
2. The model is deliberately conservative in one place: applying 2–6% MAU-level conversion to *retained* users understates their LTV, because nearly all payers are retained users. If **15% of D30-retained users** eventually pay at $25 ARPPU: LTV = 0.15 × 25 × 12 = $45 → max CAC $15/retained user → max CPI $0.90 (D30 6%) to $2.25 (D30 15%) — the last figure finally reaches real-world CPI. **Paid UA only works if you achieve both well-above-average retention and strong payer conversion among retained users.**
3. Practical implication for In Range's NYC/DMV launch: budget paid ads for *density seeding and measurement*, not as the growth engine; the economics force referral loops, campus/venue/scene-level organic, and press — consistent with how Hinge (word-of-mouth) and Pure (organic/registration growth) actually grew. Proximity apps specifically need geographic density before day-30 retention is even measurable, which further weakens broad paid social.
4. Watch the payer-lifetime assumption: 12 months is generous for dating (successful users churn out); if true average payer lifetime is 6 months, halve every LTV and max-CAC figure above. I found no public dating-payer-lifetime disclosure to pin this — flag as the model's biggest unsourced assumption.
